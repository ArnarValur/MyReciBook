# Tasty (BuzzFeed) — Product, Features & Pricing

_Researched: 2026-09-01_

Scope: product, features, pricing, business model. Reviews/sentiment are covered by a separate dossier.

**Headline:** Tasty is **not** abandoned — but it is a shrinking asset inside a company that changed hands in May 2026 and cut 35% of staff in July 2026. The Android app specifically is a **second-class, one-generation-behind build** (v2.x vs iOS v3.x) shipping ~2 releases a year. The tooling stops hard at "browse, watch, save, buy groceries." There is no personal recipe store, no import, no pantry, no diary, no offline ownership.

## 2. Positioning

- Official pitch: *"The official home of Tasty, the world's largest food video and recipe network"* (Play) and *"your ultimate cooking companion… from the internet's favorite food brand"* (App Store).
- Self-description in advertising materials: **"The Largest Digital Community for Home Cooks"** / *"Where home cooks come to play with their food."* ([advertise.buzzfeed.com/tasty](https://advertise.buzzfeed.com/tasty))
- Target user: mainstream, non-expert home cooks. **31% of the audience are parents**; **1 in 2 US Millennials (25–44) reached monthly**.
- The app is a **funnel endpoint**, not the product. The product is the video network: **187M followers** across Instagram/TikTok/Facebook/YouTube and **484M monthly cross-platform views**. tasty.co and the app exist to convert that reach into owned audience, ad inventory and affiliate/commerce transactions.
- Strategically confirmed by the Q2 2026 disclosure that BuzzFeed is pushing traffic away from platforms: direct + internal referrals are now **65% of US traffic** on owned properties, up from 61% the prior quarter.

**The tension worth noting:** the meal planner is simultaneously the paid subscription hook *and* a CPG advertising placement. Users pay $3.99/mo for a plan that BuzzFeed also sells to brands as product placement. That is a structurally compromised premium product, and it is the only thing behind the paywall.

**Ad SDK note:** AppBrain's static analysis reports **"Ads: NO"** for the Android APK (no ad-network SDK detected), yet Apple's listing declares "Contains Advertising" and coarse location for third-party advertising. The likely reading: ads are served via BuzzFeed's own web/ad stack inside webviews rather than a bundled third-party ad SDK. Flagging the discrepancy rather than resolving it. **[UNVERIFIED]**

## 5. Commerce and data

**Grocery delivery — two partners, two surfaces:**

- **Walmart** (since 2019, via the **Northfork** platform): "Add items to grocery bag" → hands off to Walmart Grocery for pickup or same-day delivery. Geo-mapped to the nearest store; users can swap by price, brand, quantity, organic. **Still the partner shown on tasty.co web** ("Build your cart with Tasty, then choose how you want to get your order from Walmart"), observed 2026-09-01. ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/walmart-cooks-up-shoppable-recipes-with-buzzfeeds-tasty-videos/561248/), [Progressive Grocer](https://progressivegrocer.com/walmart-offering-shoppable-recipes-tasty-app))
- **Instacart** (launched **Aug 28–31, 2025**, via the Instacart Developer Platform): the in-app flow. Pick servings → cost estimate → choose a nearby store from a list (Fairway, Key Food, SuperFresh, etc.) → deselect what you already have → pantry-staple prompts → per-item brand swaps → checkout in Instacart. Delivery in as little as 30 min. Explicitly stated to be live on **both iOS and Android**. ([BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/claudiasantos/instacart-recipe-shopping-tasty-app))

This is genuinely the best-built part of the product and the hardest thing for a solo developer to copy — it is a commercial partnership, not a feature.

**Merchandise / licensing:**
- Tasty cookware, bakeware, kitchen gadgets and accessories, exclusive to **Walmart** since March 2018 — **4M+ units** sold as of reporting.
- Tasty cookbooks with **Clarkson Potter** (Penguin Random House), NYT-bestselling.

**Smart kitchen — dead:**
- **Tasty One Top**, a Bluetooth smart induction cooktop, launched **Jul 27, 2017** (BuzzFeed Product Labs + GE Appliances FirstBuild), later manufactured/sold as **Tasty™ One Top® by Cuisinart**. It is now filed under **Cuisinart's Discontinued → "Tasty by Cuisinart"** category and listed **Out Of Stock**. ([Cuisinart](https://www.cuisinart.com/discontinued-the-tasty-one-top/842750112707.html)) A separate "One Top" companion app still sits in BuzzFeed's App Store portfolio, apparently orphaned. **Hardware/appliance integration is a closed chapter for Tasty.**

**Nutrition data:** real but minimal — 6 estimated macro fields per recipe, explicitly labelled estimates, no sodium or micronutrients. Fit for a label, not for tracking.

## 6. Content model

- **Recipes are written by BuzzFeed staff.** Bylines are Tasty Team members (e.g. "Robin Broadfoot, Tasty Team"), with source attribution where adapted ("Inspired by feelingfoodish.com"). A **Creator Network** of hand-picked external creators supplies social content.
- **Community submission exists but is a rights transfer, not a personal library.** `tasty.co/submit-recipe` asks you to hand over title, servings, ingredients, directions, a photo and your name/email, under the **BuzzFeed Materials Release**, for possible publication on Tasty's website. You are donating content to BuzzFeed, not storing your own. Verified 2026-09-01.
- **Current publishing velocity — measured, not claimed.** The 20 most recent recipes on tasty.co carry `datePublished` values spanning **2026-07-28 → 2026-08-31 (34 days)** = **~4.1 new recipes per week**. Within that window the pace visibly halves: ~5/week before Aug 18, ~2.5/week after — consistent with the July 22 layoffs landing, though 20 items is a small sample. (Measured directly from tasty.co structured data, 2026-09-01.)
- **Versus peak:** Digiday reported Tasty's global channels producing **~120 original videos per month** (excluding translations) around 2020, with the four-person UK team alone doing 6–10 videos/week. Not a like-for-like metric (videos vs published recipes), but the direction is unambiguous: output is a fraction of what it was.
- **New content is still being added in 2026** — confirmed. Plus a 2026 slate of new shows was announced in Dec 2025, and longform returned (Tasty 101; *You Are What You Eat*, debuted Jun 2025).
- Content is **not localised** — English only in the app, though metric units are provided and the WhatsApp channel is **97% non-US**, indicating large unserved international demand.

## 8. Gaps and weaknesses — where the tooling stops

These are structural, not backlog items. Tasty is a media brand's distribution surface; a personal cookbook is a different product with a different business model.

1. **You cannot put your own recipe in it.** The only path for a personal recipe is `submit-recipe`, which asks you to sign a materials release and give it to BuzzFeed. Grandma's recipe card has no home in Tasty. This is the single biggest gap.
2. **No import from anywhere.** No URL import, no share-sheet capture, no photo/OCR, no clipper. Every recipe a user loves that lives on another site is invisible to Tasty. A media company has an editorial incentive *never* to build this.
3. **No pantry inventory.** The Instacart flow lets you deselect what you already own — and forgets it the moment you leave the recipe. Nothing persists.
4. **No food diary, no nutrition tracking.** Six estimated macros displayed on a recipe page. No log, no history, no daily totals, no sodium, no micronutrients.
5. **No real shopping list.** There is a per-recipe cart hand-off to a retailer. There is no persistent, editable, multi-recipe list you own — and the list is designed to end at a checkout, not at your kitchen.
6. **Meal planning is editorial, paid, and an ad slot.** Curated weekly collections, not a planner. You cannot schedule your own recipes. And BuzzFeed sells brand integrations inside the same plans subscribers pay for.
7. **No offline ownership.** Video-first, account-gated, cloud-dependent, no advertised offline mode. If BuzzFeed sunsets the app or your account breaks, your saves are gone — and users are already reporting exactly that after a forced sign-up change.
8. **No data portability.** Account deletion is irreversible and wipes saved recipes and tips. No export.
9. **Ads and tracking are the product.** 8 ad slots on a single recipe page, a Nielsen measurement SDK, cross-app tracking declared in Apple's privacy label, coarse location shared for third-party advertising. A one-time-purchase app with no ads and no tracking is a categorically different proposition — and it is a proposition you can state plainly in a store listing.
10. **Android is the neglected platform.** v2.x vs iOS v3.x, two releases in 2026, a store description that has not been rewritten since roughly 2018 and still claims "over 3000 recipes", and no confirmation that the flagship AI assistant ever shipped there. **This is the #1 Food & Drink Android category, and the incumbent is not defending it.**
11. **English only.** 97% of the WhatsApp audience is outside the US, and there is no localisation.
12. **Library is smaller than advertised and barely growing.** 7,500 recipes, ~4 added per week and slowing. A library that plateaus is a library a personal cookbook eventually beats, because the user's own recipes are infinite.
13. **Owner risk.** A leveraged majority owner ($100M note), −21.8% revenue, negative EBITDA, 35% of staff cut in July 2026, and a track record of selling food brands (Complex, First We Feast). Tasty is plausibly the next thing on the block — and an acquirer buying it for the *video network* has little reason to invest in the *app*.

## 9. What we should steal

**Steal outright — cheap and high-leverage:**

1. **"% would make again"** instead of star ratings. It is a better signal for a cooking app (did it work?) and it reads warmer than stars. Trivial to implement.
2. **Metric and imperial side-by-side on every line**, not a unit toggle. Tasty has done this since 2018 and it removes a whole class of friction for a non-US audience — which is exactly MyReciBook's audience.
3. **Keep the screen awake in cook mode.** A one-line feature Tasty markets as a headline benefit because users genuinely care.
4. **Cost estimate per recipe / per serving.** Users volunteer praise for this unprompted in Play reviews. With a pantry and a grocery list, MyReciBook can do it *better* — estimate only what you actually need to buy.
5. **Deselect-what-you-already-have before shopping.** Tasty does this per-recipe and forgets. MyReciBook has a pantry, so it can do it automatically and persistently. Take the interaction, keep the state.
6. **Filter taxonomy.** Cuisine / Dietary / Meal / Seasonal / Appliance / Cooking Style is a well-worn, tested set — no need to reinvent it.
7. **Tips with helpful-votes, sortable by most-helpful.** The mechanic is free; the volume is not.
8. **The Remix idea, inverted.** "Make it easier" / "make it more nutritious" / "surprise me" is a great AI affordance. On MyReciBook it should operate on **the user's own recipes** — which is something Tasty structurally cannot do, because it does not have any.

**Only possible with a media budget — do not chase:**

- **484M monthly views and 187M followers.** Tasty's discovery engine is a decade of video production and a newsroom. Do not compete on top-of-funnel reach; compete on what happens after the user has already found a recipe.
- **Instacart/Walmart checkout integration.** These are commercial partnerships with retail-media economics behind them. A grocery *list* that exports cleanly (share sheet, plain text, copy-to-clipboard, deep link) gets 80% of the value at 0% of the deal-making.
- **A staff culinary team producing original recipes and video.** MyReciBook's content is the *user's* content. That is the whole point, and it is also why the library never plateaus.
- **A branded creator network and show sponsorships.** Different business entirely — and it is the business that is currently losing money.

**The positioning gap to occupy:** Tasty is *someone else's* recipes, rented, with ads, on a platform that just cut a third of its staff. MyReciBook is *your* recipes, owned, paid once, no ads, offline, exportable. The one-time $25 purchase is not just a pricing choice against a $3.99/mo subscription — it is the credible answer to "what happens to my saved recipes when the media company gets sold again?"
