# The Recipe App Category — What Seven Competitors Actually Look Like Underneath

_Researched 2026-09-01. Sources: the fourteen dossiers in this folder._

This started because the Play Store "recipes" results look intimidating — millions of downloads, glossy screenshots, 4.7-star badges. Fourteen agents went underneath those listings. What they found is a category that is **large, wealthy, badly run, and structurally unable to give people what they keep asking for.**

---

## 1. The star ratings are lying, and we can prove it

This is the single most important finding, so it goes first.

Google Play and the App Store display a **recency-weighted average dominated by silent tappers** — people who hit five stars in a prompt without typing anything. When the research agents pulled the *written* reviews instead, the picture inverted:

| App | Displayed rating | Text-review reality |
|---|---|---|
| **ReciMe** | 4.7★ / 97.2K | **3.44★** across 4,754 text reviews — 28.6% one-star |
| **Mob** | 4.6★ (Play GB), #1 top-grossing UK | **2.26★** across 404 text reviews — **52% one-star** |
| **Cookpad** (global listing) | 4.4★ GB | 11% one-star in GB; newest 60 reviews average 4.03 vs 4.57 lifetime |
| **Tasty** | 4.7★ / 174K | Newest 320 reviews average 4.58; 1★ at 6.3% vs 2.3% lifetime |
| **Foodvisor** | 4.7★ / 194K | 1★ (4,866) outnumbers 2★ (2,894) by 1.7:1 — an anger spike, not drift |

Mob's split is the cleanest demonstration: 1,408 star-only ratings average 4.65, while the 404 people who actually wrote something average 2.26. **Anyone who cares enough to type is unhappy.**

And SideChef's "4.0" isn't a rating at all — Play localises ratings per storefront, and Norway's histogram resolves to roughly **five people**. Its US rating is 4.5 and its German rating is 3.8. The 4.0 measures Norwegian absence, not quality.

**What this means for morale, stated plainly:** the shine is legacy install bases, media budgets and onboarding rating prompts. It is not user love. ReciMe's five-star bucket contains 9.5% reviews written *before the person used the app* ("just downloaded", "so far so good") because they prompt on onboarding. We are not competing against 4.7-star products. We are competing against 4.7-star *badges* worn by 3.4-star products.

## 2. Nobody sells a one-time price. Not one of the seven.

| App | What a year costs | Norway |
|---|---|---|
| **Mob** | £39.99 / €49.99 / **499 kr** / $59.99 | 499 kr **every year** |
| **Tasty** | Free + ads; Tasty+ $34.99/yr | — |
| **Foodvisor** | $83.99/yr headline US (also 3 concurrent monthlies, $14.99–$39.99); €59.99/yr FR | ~NOK 650–800 `[UNVERIFIED]` |
| **SideChef** | $49.99/yr | No NOK price — quotes USD in the Norwegian listing |
| **ReciMe** | $39.99/yr (actively testing $29.99 / $59.99) | Billed NOK, price unpublished |
| **Cookpad** | ¥308/mo Japan; ~$5/mo global `[UNVERIFIED]` | **Premium is not sold in Norway at all** |
| **COOKmate** | €20/yr web, $22.99/yr iOS | No NOK price published |

**MyReciBook at ~$25 once is roughly 260 kr.** A Norwegian Mob subscriber pays nearly twice that annually, forever, for a closed English catalogue with UK supermarket assumptions.

Three of the seven **deliberately refuse to publish a price.** Foodvisor's help centre says outright it cannot give a fixed universal price; it runs ten concurrent App Store SKUs spanning 3.5×. ReciMe runs ten SKUs across three price points. Mob says price "can vary… depending on region, current promotions, and platform fees." A category where nobody will tell you the price is a category with an enormous trust vacancy in it.

### Users are already writing our listing copy

Unprompted, across four different apps, in their own reviews:

> "If it were $20.00 for a one time purchase I would definitely be in." — COOKmate, Play

> "no possibility to purchase the app once and for all" — SideChef, 68 helpful votes

> "Would be worth it for a one time payment" / "Treat it like a cookbook and have a download fee" — Mob

> "or buy some great cook books" — **the most-upvoted review on Mob's entire Play listing**, 45 votes

ReciMe's unhappy users name Paprika's one-time price as the reason they leave. Roughly half of ReciMe's negative reviews are about monetisation rather than the product — 20.2% subscriptions as a concept, 16.0% "it isn't actually free", 14.6% price level.

**We do not have to educate this market. It is already asking, out loud, with vote counts, for exactly what we are building.** And no incumbent can answer without breaking its own revenue model.

## 3. Three of our five pillars are simply unoccupied

| | Own recipes | Import | Pantry | **Food diary** | Nutrition calc | Export | Offline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Foodvisor** | ✖ flat favourites | ✖ | ✖ | ✔ (its whole point) | ✔ | ✖ | ✖ |
| **COOKmate** | ✔ | ✔ 200+ scrapers | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ text blob, won't rescale | ✔ best in class | ✔ |
| **Tasty** | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ | ~6 estimated macros | ✖ | ✖ |
| **Cookpad** | ✔ metered | ✔ metered | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ |
| **ReciMe** | ✔ | ✔ best in class | ✖ | ✖ | beta, per-recipe only | ✖ no bulk export | partial |
| **SideChef** | manual only | ✖ none at all | ✔ | ✖ | ✔ free %DV | ✖ | ✖ |
| **Mob** | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ | ✖ | printed, then discarded | ✖ | ✖ |
| **MyReciBook** | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |

Read the food-diary column again. **One app has a daily food log, and it is a calorie counter, not a recipe app.** Read the pantry column. One app has a pantry, and it hasn't shipped a release since March 2025.

**Nobody in this category has combined a personal cookbook with a pantry and a diary.** That is not a gap we found by squinting. It is an entire column of empty boxes.

The reason is instructive and worth understanding: media brands (Tasty, Mob) can't let you own recipes because their catalogue is their only asset. UGC platforms (Cookpad) can't, because metering your own cookbook *is* the revenue. Calorie counters (Foodvisor) don't want recipes, they want logged grams. Nobody is structurally positioned to do all of it — except an app whose customer is the cook.

## 4. Data ownership is the category's open wound

- **Tasty**: no export, no print, no copy. Account deletion irreversibly wipes your saves. Users are currently losing recipes to an unfixed Facebook-login regression running since ~April 2026.
- **Cookpad**: the UGC licence lets them commercially exploit and sublicense your recipes for free, waives your moral rights, and survives your account's termination — while the Terms tell you that backing up your content is *your* responsibility. There is no export feature.
- **ReciMe**: no bulk export.
- **Mob**: closed catalogue, nothing is yours.
- **COOKmate**: automatic daily local `.mcb` backup to a user-visible file, plus a **published XML schema**. The best data-ownership story in the category — and, not coincidentally, the app that has survived sixteen years on 4.6 stars.

Meanwhile the loudest churn events across every dossier are variations of *"I lost my recipes."* COOKmate users on device change. Tasty users on the login break. SideChef users when Budget Bytes shut down and the notice said their recipes "will not be transferred." Mob users after changing phone.

**"Your recipes are files on your device, and you can take them with you"** is a verifiable claim that exactly one competitor can make, and it is not one of the big ones.

### The unguarded door: migration *in*

Users with 500–1500 recipes are trapped by **file formats, not loyalty.** Several COOKmate reviewers report retyping recipes by hand. Nobody in this category has built a good importer for a *rival's* export. Ingesting COOKmate, Paprika and Recipe Keeper libraries with images and formatting intact takes the category's most valuable users at the exact moment they are angriest.

## 5. The incumbents are wobbling — with receipts

| App | State |
|---|---|
| **Tasty** | BuzzFeed sold ~51% to Byron Allen's family office May 2026 ($120M). July 2026: **35% of staff cut, including Tasty**. Q2 revenue −21.8%. | **SideChef** | **No release on either platform since 2025-03-14** — ~18 months — while tightening its paywall *server-side* and still wearing a Play Editors' Choice badge. Engineering follows the B2B payers; the payers aren't the users. |
| **Cookpad** | Down to **78 employees globally**. Revenue falling five straight quarters. Operating profit −98% YoY. Premium unbuyable across the Nordics, Germany and the Netherlands. |

Only **ReciMe** is genuinely growing and genuinely dangerous — and it is spending 2026 moving features people already had (unit converter, print, guided cook mode) *behind* the paywall, while its Play listing still advertises them as plain features. Its Android build is v4.1.2 against iOS v6.1.5, its listing tells Android users the app syncs to "iOS and iPad", and both of its lifetime giveaways were iOS-only. **Android is second-class at our most direct competitor, and Android is the category we are entering.**

## 6. Norway and the Nordics are unserved. Completely.

- **Cookpad**: 25 languages, **none Nordic**. Premium not sold in any Nordic country. 51 lifetime iOS ratings in Norway, zero in Norwegian.
- **COOKmate**: ships Danish and Finnish, **no Norwegian**. No Norway storefront presence. Two Norwegian import sites. A live German decimal-separator bug.
- **Tasty**: **English only in every storefront on earth.** Nordic App Store ratings are its lowest worldwide (FI 4.77, DK 4.78, SE 4.82).
- **SideChef**: English and German only. No Nordic language, no European grocery integration, and it quotes **USD prices in the Norwegian listing** while advertising one-click checkout from Instacart/Walmart/Target with no US-only caveat.
- **Mob**: English only, UK aisles and ingredients — while charging **499 kr/yr** in Norway.
- **ReciMe**: no Nordic languages; 292 Norwegian iOS ratings in total.

Every one of these companies treats Norway as a billing currency rather than a market. We live here. Norwegian and Icelandic localisation, metric-native units, and Nordic ingredient names are cheap for us and uncontested by everyone.

## 7. Rules we're taking from other people's mistakes

**On pricing**
1. **Never ship a "trial."** Trial mechanics are the single largest complaint category across Mob, ReciMe, Foodvisor and SideChef. Mob runs two different trial lengths (5 days via Instagram, 7 in-app) and it costs them dearly. A one-time price makes trials unnecessary.
2. **Make restore-purchase flawless.** Under one-time pricing this is existential, not a nicety.
3. **Never remove or paywall something people already have.** COOKmate killed its $5.99 one-time in July 2022 and its users still haven't forgiven it — 790 one-star reviews, more than 2★ and 3★ combined. Cookpad's Terms explicitly reserve the right to start charging for previously-free features, and its top one-star review (215 upvotes) is exactly that. ReciMe did it in mid-2026. Mob did it in mid-2025.
4. **Publish the price.** Three of seven refuse to. Say "$25, once, forever" in the first line of the listing.

**On product**
5. **Never lose a user's data, in any migration, ever.** This is the #1 churn trigger in every single dossier.
6. **Metric is a unit system, not a display toggle.** SideChef converts to "237mL of rice" and it costs them more stars than not converting at all.
7. **If you ask someone a preference, honour it everywhere.** SideChef's most-repeated complaint, unfixed from 2018 to 2026, is that its allergy and diet filters are ignored. Mob's unnormalised ingredients break allergen filtering — one reviewer called it "dangerous." **This is a safety issue and a free win.**
8. **Never market a feature that only works in one country.**
9. **Never score a food as "bad."** Foodvisor's red/frowny-face scoring draws accusations of feeding diet culture. A recipe-and-pantry app that never moralises about food has a clean, defensible position — and it's ours for free.
10. **Don't meter import, and always keep the source link.** Cookpad monetised imports above 5/week in March 2026 and drew a public revolt from Japanese food creators. ReciMe caps free users at 5/week and it's their most-complained-about wall.

**The one real threat to our model** — and this deserves respect rather than optimism. COOKmate did not fail at one-time pricing. It failed because one-time pricing had to fund a **permanently recurring cost**: 200+ hand-maintained site scrapers plus cloud sync. The developer said so publicly in 2018. Our $25 survives only if our recurring costs stay bounded — parse schema.org/JSON-LD generically rather than maintaining a parser per site, keep storage device-local, take on no server-sync obligation we can't fund forever. **This is the constraint that decides whether the business works.** Worth its own session.

## 8. Things worth stealing outright

- **ReciMe's import cascade** — caption → audio transcription → find the original website, cheapest path first, source link always preserved. Their founder scrapped the entire app to rebuild around this one feature and tripled retention.
- **ReciMe's kitchen micro-details** — ingredient quantities highlighted inline *inside* the method steps, tap-to-convert oven temperatures, screen stays awake in cook mode.
- **SideChef's guided cooking** — a photo or video at *every step*, not per recipe. Plus voice-advanced hands-free mode with step-bound timers, and "% of ingredient used" for leftovers.
- **Mob's Adapt Recipe** — swap an ingredient, raise the protein, cut the calories, convert to a dietary variant, switch it to the air fryer.
- **Mob's aisle-sorted shopping list**, and their abandoned **cost-per-portion** position ("feed 4 for under £10") — a proven angle sitting vacant.
- **Cookpad's ingredient-first search** across your own saved collection — the most-loved feature in their entire corpus, in every language.
- **COOKmate's data model** — daily local backup to a visible file, a published schema, and over-quota content that degrades to read-only instead of being held hostage.
- **Mob's marketing line**, which we can make more honestly than they can: *no ads, no long back stories, ingredient amounts shown in the method.*

## What this changes about the plan

Nothing about the destination. A great deal about the confidence level.

The category's incumbents are large, tired, and locked into business models that forbid them from doing the three things their own users keep asking for: **own your recipes, pay once, and track what you actually eat.** We are not late to a crowded market. We are early to an obvious one that nobody with a subscription P&L is allowed to enter.

**The open questions this raises, in priority order:**

1. **Bounded recurring cost.** Can the import path work without a maintained-parser treadmill? This decides whether $25-once is a business or a slow bleed. → own session.
2. **Migration-in.** Which rival export formats do we ingest at launch, and how do we reach those users at the moment they're angry?
3. **Nordic localisation.** Norwegian and Icelandic at launch, or v1.1?
4. **The Play listing.** We have competitors' users writing our copy for us. First line should be the price.
