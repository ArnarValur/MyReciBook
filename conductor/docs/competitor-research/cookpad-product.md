# Cookpad — Product, Features & Pricing

_Researched: 2026-09-01_

All store figures observed 2026-09-01 unless a different date is stated. Storefront-specific numbers are labelled with the storefront, because Google Play and the App Store report ratings per country.

## Snapshot

Cookpad Inc. (Tokyo Stock Exchange, code 2193) runs the world's largest user-generated recipe platform. It is also a shrinking company: revenue is down nine consecutive periods, the group employed **78 people worldwide as of 30 June 2026**, and the business now consists of a dominant Japanese franchise plus a thin international long tail. Two separate Android apps carry the Cookpad name in our market — a Japan-built app and a UK-published global app — and they are different products on different backends.

The strategic headline for MyReciBook: Cookpad's paywall is actively migrating **away** from search perks and **towards gating the personal cookbook** — unlimited saving, unlimited importing, unlimited recipe creation, custom folders, and private recipe notes are all now Premium-only in at least one storefront's copy. That is precisely the territory a one-time-purchase personal recipe app occupies, and Cookpad is charging rent on it monthly.

### The 2023–2026 retrenchment

- 2016: founder Sano, holding ~43.6% of votes, ousted president Yoshiteru Akita over the diversification strategy. CTO and senior engineers left. ([the-shashi.com](https://the-shashi.com/en/tse/2193/))
- 2017 onwards: short-form video (Kurashiru/dely, DELISH KITCHEN) broke the text-search moat. First net loss since listing in 2019; operating losses 2021, 2022, 2023.
- Feb 2023: 40 voluntary redundancies; headcount fell 409 → 147 in one year; six businesses closed. ([the-shashi.com](https://the-shashi.com/en/tse/2193/))
- Oct 2023: Sano returns as president and begins unwinding the Akita-era acquisitions — selling the overseas recipe companies, Minna no Wedding and Coach United, pulling resources back to the core recipe business. ([the-shashi.com](https://the-shashi.com/en/tse/2193/))
- Q1 FY2026: **¥185m of one-off costs for workforce reductions at overseas locations**, explicitly described as "initiatives to streamline the Group's structure". ([1H FY2026 results](https://cf.cpcdn.com/info/assets/wp-content/uploads/20260807111450/Consolidated-Financial-Results-for-the-Six-Months-Ended-June-30-2026-Under-IFRS.pdf))
- No public announcement of a specific country exit was found. What is documented is a group-wide headcount cut concentrated overseas, not a named market withdrawal. `[UNVERIFIED]` whether any individual country community has been closed.

Cookpad's Articles of Incorporation state that when everyday cooking becomes enjoyable in every household around the world, **the company will dissolve**. This is quoted verbatim in the interim filing — it is a real governing document, not marketing. ([1H FY2026 results](https://cf.cpcdn.com/info/assets/wp-content/uploads/20260807111450/Consolidated-Financial-Results-for-the-Six-Months-Ended-June-30-2026-Under-IFRS.pdf))

## Positioning

Cookpad is UGC-first and proudly editorial-free. Every recipe comes from a user; there is no test kitchen, no house voice, no standardisation. Its own Terms say so bluntly: recipes are "published by other users, like you, and not by Cookpad… Cookpad does not screen or check recipes before they are posted, so there may be some inappropriate, incorrect or unsafe recipes, ingredients or cooking methods." ([UK Terms §4](https://cookpad.com/uk/terms))

The trust mechanism is not ratings but **cooksnaps** — photos posted by other people who actually made the dish, shown under the recipe. Fifteen photos from fifteen kitchens is the review. This is Cookpad's genuine, hard-to-copy asset.

Since roughly March 2026 the global product has been repositioning from "search the world's recipes" to **"your permanent personal recipe collection, from anywhere"** — import from TikTok/Instagram/any website, OCR handwritten recipes, AI-assisted recipe writing, folders, weekly menu, auto shopping list. The Play short description changed to "Recipe app: weeknight dinners, easy chicken, save, import and cook what you love" and the iOS name changed to "Cookpad: Recipe & Meal Planner". **They are moving onto MyReciBook's ground.**

## Free vs Premium

The paywall is **in flux and inconsistent across storefronts as of 2026-09-01** — three different Premium feature lists are live simultaneously for the same app:

| Storefront copy | Premium unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| Google Play, en / en_GB / en_NZ | 1. Most popular recipes at the top of every search · 2. **Unlimited recipes saved from other home cooks** · 3. Advanced search filters |
| App Store, US / GB / ES | 1. Most popular recipes at the top of search · 2. **Unlimited importing and creating of recipes** · 3. **Organise your favourite recipes into custom folders** |
| Play "What's new", Aug 2026 | **Private personal notes on recipes — "(Premium members only)"** |
| Cookpad's own US blog, Mar/Jun 2026 | "The free plan lets you save recipes from social media and websites, with access to your Imported folder. **Unlimited saves and the ability to organise recipes into unlimited themed collections require Cookpad Premium.**" |

Read together, the free tier in 2026 is: search, read, cooksnap, follow, post *some* recipes, import *some* recipes, and one system folder called "Imported". Everything that makes it a usable personal cookbook — capacity, organisation, and annotation — is metered.

**The numeric caps are not published.** Cookpad's store copy, FAQ, blog and help centre all say "unlimited" for Premium and never state the free ceiling. A secondary source reports a **60-recipe free bookmark cap** and Japan's Premium ceiling as **3,000 recipes**, but neither figure appears in any Cookpad-controlled document I could reach, so both are `[UNVERIFIED]`. (The 3,000 figure comes from a [Harvard d3 case study](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/cookpad-spreading-the-joy-of-cooking-worldwide/) written around 2018 and is in any case historical.) Hiding the number is itself a deliberate friction design: you discover the wall by hitting it.

Japan's Premium is a different, richer product — eight distinct perks: 人気順検索 (popularity search), 殿堂入りレシピ (1,000+ cooksnap hall of fame), 絞り込み検索 (filter by cooksnap count), 似たレシピ (unlimited similar-recipe access), 話題のレシピ (trending, 100+ cooksnaps this month), 専門家厳選レシピ (expert-curated by goal — weaning, diet), デイリーアクセス数ランキング, プレミアム献立 (seasonal meal plans), plus プレミアムレシピ from authors whose recipes have been cooked 300,000+ times. ([cookpad.com/jp/premium](https://cookpad.com/jp/premium), [Japan Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cookpad.android.activities))

Structural pricing notes from the Terms ([UK Terms §§7–9](https://cookpad.com/uk/terms)):
- Fees vary by country **and** by whether you buy on web, iOS or Android.
- Web subscriptions are **pre-paid for a fixed duration and cannot be cancelled**; they simply expire.
- App subscriptions auto-renew monthly; no refunds or credits for partial periods.
- Cookpad reserves the right to raise prices "at any time in our sole discretion (including the introduction of charges for aspects of the Premium Service that were previously provided at no extra cost)" — with 30 days to cancel. That clause is exactly how "saving recipes" became a paid feature.
- EU/EEA consumers get a 14-day withdrawal right, exercised by emailing info-uk@cookpad.com with a prescribed form.

Historical anchor: Japan's Premium was **¥280/month** around 2018 ([Harvard d3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/cookpad-spreading-the-joy-of-cooking-worldwide/)) versus ¥308 today — a ~10% rise in roughly eight years, i.e. essentially just Japan's consumption-tax and inflation drift. Cookpad has never used price rises to defend revenue; it has lost subscribers instead.

## Business model and financials

Cookpad reports as a **single business segment** ("Make everyday cooking fun!") and **no longer discloses an advertising-versus-subscription revenue split**. What the filings do say, repeatedly, is that revenue declines are "mainly attributable to a decline in the number of premium service subscribers" — which tells you subscriptions are now the swing factor, and that advertising has already shrunk to a rump. The apps themselves are now ad-free in both Japan and globally.

**The 2026 strategy — "cooking as a whole":** in practice this means three things. (2) **Cookpad Mart**, a Japan-only grocery-delivery app (2.2★ on Play). (3) **"moment" by Cookpad** — an AI cooking-coaching service using multimodal AI (text, vision, audio) to teach people to cook, at [cookwithmoment.com](https://cookwithmoment.com/). Both are named in the interim filing as businesses run "alongside the recipe service business… to remove barriers that prevent people from enjoying cooking". A small tell about resourcing: the moment site is a client-side JS shell whose Open Graph image is still Lovable's default placeholder, meaning it was assembled on a no-code AI builder and shipped without swapping the social card.

- **Account required** for anything beyond reading. Guests can browse on the web only.
- **Data controller:** the UK registered branch of Cookpad Inc. **DPO** contactable at Silversmiths, 1-3 Broad Plain, Bristol BS2 0JP and info-uk@cookpad.com.
- **EEA transfers:** personal data is transferred outside the EEA, "including USA and Japan", under **EU standard contractual clauses**. Full GDPR rights enumerated (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, withdrawal of consent, direct-marketing opt-out) plus the right to complain to a national DPA.
- **Children:** no knowing collection under 16; accounts terminated if discovered.
- **Special-category data:** Cookpad explicitly acknowledges that dietary filters can reveal allergies (health) and religion (kosher/halal) and says it treats these "with extra care" — but the lawful basis for that processing is left as "may be subject to your consent".
- **Photo-library scanning:** an opt-in feature uploads *thumbnails of every image on your device* to Cookpad's servers so an image classifier can pick out food photos. Non-food thumbnails are then deleted. Disclosed in both the Terms (§13) and the Privacy Policy.
- **Staleness:** the Privacy Policy was **last updated 1 February 2022** and the Terms of Service are **valid from 1 November 2018**. Both still describe advertising networks and personalised advertising partners, in an app whose own store listing now says "AD-FREE". The legal layer has not kept pace with the product. Note also the split-brain on which policy applies: the global *Play* listing points to `cookpad.com/us/privacy` while the UK *web* uses `cookpad.com/uk/privacy` and the Japan app points to `cookpad.com/jp/privacy`.

**Who owns your recipes (UK Terms §10):**

> "We do not claim ownership of any recipes, photographs, graphics, comments, information, data, text or other materials you upload or post."

Then, immediately after, you grant Cookpad a **worldwide, royalty-free, on-going and transferable licence** — sublicensable to affiliates, third parties and other users — to "use, copy, reproduce, process, adapt, modify, translate, publish, transmit, display and distribute" your content or derivatives, in any medium now known or later developed, "for any and all of our business activities". The licence explicitly covers **commercial exploitation with no compensation to you**, and you **waive your moral rights** (except in Spain, where that waiver is deleted and the licence is limited to the statutory rights term).

There is no stated expiry and no clause returning the licence on account deletion. Section 18 goes further: suspension or termination "could result in a permanent loss of access to and use of certain aspects of the services, **including your own uploaded Content**, even if you later re-register". And §14: "you are solely responsible for securing and backing up your Content" — with no export or backup feature offered anywhere in the product.

**Imported recipes are the exception in your favour:** recipes you import from TikTok, Instagram or the web are private, marked as imported, and *cannot* be published publicly. ([Cookpad blog](https://blog.cookpad.com/us/cookpad-recipe-import-feature-save-recipes-from-anywhere/))

## Gaps and weaknesses

1. **The non-Japanese communities are far thinner than the branding implies — and Cookpad's own UI proves it.** The "Hall of Fame" threshold is **1,000+ cooksnaps in Japan, more than 10 in Spain, and more than 5 in the UK**. A UK recipe cooked by six people is a hall-of-famer. That single design compromise, visible on their public Premium pages, is the whole story of the international business. Backed by iOS ratings: 672,776 in Japan versus 838 in the UK.
2. **No nutrition data anywhere in the global product.** No calories, no macros, no per-serving analysis. Calorie display existed historically as a Japan Premium perk and is not in the current Japanese Premium list. For an app being renamed "Recipe & Meal Planner", this is a hole.
3. **No food diary, no intake tracking, no goals.** Nothing that connects cooking to eating.
4. **No pantry inventory.** "Cook with what you already have in your fridge or pantry" is a *search prompt* — you type ingredients each time. Nothing is stored, nothing depletes, nothing warns you what is about to spoil despite food-waste reduction being a stated selling point.
5. **No offline access.** The product is built around a live community feed and expects a connection for browsing and for other cooks' photos. A kitchen with bad signal breaks it.
6. **Metered personal cookbook.** Saving others' recipes is capped, importing is capped, creating recipes is capped, folders beyond "Imported" are Premium, and private notes are Premium. And the caps are unpublished, so you cannot plan around them.
7. **Premium is unavailable across the Nordics, Germany, the Netherlands and most of northern Europe.** Users in those markets hit the caps with no upgrade path — a hard wall with no door, and a large served-but-unmonetised audience.
8. **Recipe quality variance is structural and admitted.** Cookpad's Terms warn that recipes may be "inappropriate, incorrect or unsafe" and advise inexperienced cooks to seek instruction elsewhere. Quantities drift between grams and handfuls; method depth is uneven; there is no recommendation, only a wall of plausible results.
9. **No unit conversion.** A UK reviewer's complaint about US cups versus grams (30 July 2026) drew a support reply asking for more detail rather than pointing at a feature — because there isn't one. In a platform whose whole premise is cooking across borders, that is a notable miss.
10. **Import is slow.** Cookpad's own documentation says a single import takes 1–2 minutes and recommends enabling notifications so you know when it finishes.
11. **No export, no backup, and a licence that outlives your account.** You cannot get your own cookbook out. Cookpad tells you you're responsible for backing it up while giving you no way to do so.
12. **Login friction.** Account required to save anything, plus a live Facebook-login bug that strands users in a browser (reported 29 July 2026, still drawing an apology-and-email reply from Cookpad on 24 Aug 2026).
13. **A company shrinking under the product.** 78 employees globally, five straight quarters of falling revenue, operating profit down 98% year-on-year, no forecast published, capital being returned via buybacks rather than invested. Feature velocity is currently high, but it is being produced by a very small team that just cut its overseas staff.
14. **Two apps, three packages, inconsistent store copy.** Three different Premium feature lists are live simultaneously for the same app on different storefronts. Users in Europe can be served an India-targeted listing for a Japanese app. Brand confusion is real and self-inflicted.

## What we should steal — and what we cannot

**Steal:**

- **Cooksnaps as the trust primitive.** Photos from real kitchens under a recipe beat star ratings, and they cost nothing to host. Even in a paid single-player app, "photos of my own attempts, dated, attached to the recipe" is a powerful personal feature — a cooking log — and it's the seed of any community we might later add.
- **Ingredient-first search over *your own* collection.** Cookpad's genuinely best idea is that a saved recipe becomes searchable by ingredient, not just title. Type "salmon" and get every recipe in your library containing salmon. That should be table stakes in MyReciBook and it pairs perfectly with a real pantry, which Cookpad lacks.
- **One-tap import from TikTok/Instagram/any website, with the content copied, not linked** — so the recipe survives the creator deleting it. Plus auto-translation and OCR of handwritten cards and cookbook pages. This is the strongest part of their 2026 product and it is exactly what a personal cookbook is for. Beat them on speed: theirs takes 1–2 minutes.
- **Keep the original source link attached** to an imported recipe so you can go back and watch the technique.
- **Cook mode: screen stays awake, one step full-screen, readable from across the counter.** Cheap to build, disproportionately loved.
- **"Cook Today" pin list that resets each morning.** A tiny, well-judged interaction — a daily working set distinct from a permanent library.
- **Challenges as a lightweight engagement loop** (their Apron Challenge runs annually and drives posting).
- **Their pricing wall as our headline.** Cookpad charges monthly, forever, for unlimited saves, unlimited imports, folders and private notes — and won't even sell it in Norway. Every one of those is a bullet in a one-time-purchase pitch. "The things they meter, you own."
- **Their legal posture as our contrast.** Cookpad's licence lets them commercially exploit your recipes for free, sublicense them, and keep them if your account is terminated — with no export feature. "Your recipes stay yours, and you can export them any time" is a real, verifiable differentiator, not marketing.

**Impossible for us at our size, and we should not try:**

- **The UGC corpus.** 10M+ recipes accumulated over 28 years, with SEO to match. Do not attempt a public recipe database.
- **The Japanese network effect.** 672,776 iOS ratings and #1 top-grossing in Food & Drink is not addressable.
- **Popularity ranking as a paid feature.** It only has value when millions of people have cooked the same recipe. We will never have that signal, so we should not build a product that depends on it — which is fortunate, because it points us at the private cookbook instead.
- **A social graph.** Followers and network feeds need liquidity we won't have. Build the single-player experience to be excellent alone.

The strategic read: Cookpad's international arm is a **large, well-known, unmonetisable audience being served by a shrinking company with 78 employees**, whose product is drifting into personal-cookbook territory while metering the exact features that make a personal cookbook work — and which cannot sell that upgrade in Norway or anywhere else in the Nordics. That is a gap, not a wall.
