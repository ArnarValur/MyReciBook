## 2. Positioning and marketing

**The pitch:** "World's most popular recipe organizer. All your recipes, on your phone." Store subtitle: "#1 app to save recipes from Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube & more!" Help centre framing: "your all-in-one recipe and meal-planning sidekick."

**Deliberate anti-positioning.** ReciMe does **not** publish its own recipes and has **no discovery feed**. Christine Nguyen: "When we first released ReciMe, we built too many things, and none of them worked well… As soon as we realised that our smart importer was our most loved feature, we scrapped everything and re-built our entire app just to drive that one core action." That single-action focus is the whole strategy.

**Target user:** the social-media-native home cook drowning in saved Instagram/TikTok folders and camera-roll screenshots. Skews female (the app's own review-quote selection leans that way, and its most-quoted App Store review is titled "For the organization girlies"). App Store genres are **Food & Drink + Social Networking** — they still classify themselves as social.

**ASO keyword spine** (from titles/subtitles/description): recipe organizer, save recipes, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube, Pinterest, meal planner, grocery list, cookbook, calories. Play title: "ReciMe: Recipes & Meal Planner". They stack the five source-platform brand names into every field they control — that's the keyword play.

### The growth playbook (document this — it's the transferable asset)

1. **Give away lifetime entitlements to bargain communities to buy chart rank and reviews.** The founder personally posted on OzBargain as `homecookingaddict` on 2023-09-05 and again 2024-01-22, offering a **free lifetime subscription** (unlocked by a deliberate five-times-open-and-close-the-paywall easter egg, not an IAP). Result, per a community member: **"They went from #150s to #6 in the food & drink category in a couple days with 0 marketing expenses."** ([OzBargain 798026](https://www.ozbargain.com.au/node/798026), [OzBargain 827499](https://www.ozbargain.com.au/node/827499)). She also ran a **90% off** promo four days before the first giveaway.
2. **Found-in-thread product research.** In both threads she answered nearly every comment, logged feature requests live ("adding to feedback slack channel and backlog right now"), and named competitors' gaps openly. Cheap, high-signal discovery.
3. **Multi-account TikTok brand.** Reported 100,000+ followers across multiple TikTok accounts; TikTok used across the whole funnel (discovery → acquisition → retention). `@recime.app` posts feature-demo shorts (e.g. the fridge-ingredients video). `[UNVERIFIED]` — no public spend or ROAS figures.
4. **Creator partnerships programme.** A "Creator Partnerships" link sits in the site footer (page currently returns empty to fetch) — they court the same recipe creators whose content they scrape.
5. **Owned Facebook community** — `facebook.com/groups/recimeapp`, "thousands of passionate ReciMe cooks," pushed in the onboarding help article.
6. **Programmatic SEO farm.** `recime.app/blog` is an off-topic content mill: "Best Calculator Apps", "Best Free Photo Editing App for iPhone", "Top Picks for the Best Day Trading App in 2025", "The Best Intermittent Fasting Apps". This is domain-authority farming, not audience building.
7. **Mainstream TV placement.** Site badges "As featured on" **The Drew Barrymore Show** and **CBS**.

## 4. Free vs paid — exactly where the wall is

Authoritative source: [What are the benefits of ReciMe Plus?](https://recime.app/help/en/articles/11630603-what-are-the-benefits-of-recime-plus) (last updated **2026-08-16**) and [Is there a free version?](https://recime.app/help/en/articles/11596201-is-there-a-free-version-of-the-app) (2026-08-09).

**Free tier gets:**
- **5 recipe imports per week** (rolling weekly allowance, not a lifetime cap)
- Manual recipe writing and saving
- Cookbooks
- Weekly meal plan
- Smart grocery lists with aisle sorting
- Grocery Checkout (order online)
- Screen-stays-on while cooking

**ReciMe Plus unlocks:**
- Unlimited imports from Instagram / TikTok / Facebook / websites
- Cookbook + handwritten-note scanning (OCR)
- Nutrition Calculator (calories, protein, carbs, fat) — *beta*
- Copy-and-paste whole recipe blocks
- **Step-by-step guided Cooking Mode**
- **Measurement Converter** (metric ⇄ imperial)
- **"Ask ReciMe" AI assistant**
- **Export to PDF / print**

**What breaks at the wall:** the sixth import of the week. That's the wall by design — importing *is* the product, and 5/week is roughly one Sunday scroll session.

**The paywall has hardened over time.** Compare:
- Jan 2025 (Plan to Eat): free plan capped at **eight** smart imports total; scaler and converters described as free.
- May 2026 (RecipeOne): scaler, cook mode and converters still described as free tools.
- Aug 2026 (ReciMe's own help centre): **measurement converter, step-by-step cooking mode, export/print and the AI assistant are all Plus.**

They are steadily moving utilities behind the wall. That is a competitive opening, not an accident.

**Also note:** on the free plan, imported recipes historically defaulted to **public**; only manually-uploaded recipes had a private toggle (founder, Jan 2024). Third-party reviews still claim "no private recipes on free." `[UNVERIFIED for 2026]` but worth probing.

**IAP price points visible on the US App Store listing on 2026-09-01:** ten "ReciMe Plus" SKUs at **$29.99, $39.99 (×6), $59.99 (×3)**. ### The lifetime question — the direct answer

**ReciMe has no lifetime tier and never has had one as a retail SKU.** The two "Free Lifetime Subscription" OzBargain posts were the *founder* giving away a server-side entitlement via a hidden five-taps-on-the-paywall easter egg, headlined against the AU$59.99 annual price. There is no purchasable one-time price anchor from ReciMe.

**What this means for MyReciBook's ~$25 one-time:**
- Our price is **~62% of their first year** at $39.99, and **~42%** at $59.99.
- Against AU$7.99/month, $25 is break-even at about **3–4 months** of their monthly plan.
- Because they have no lifetime SKU, **we do not have to beat a lifetime price — we get to define the category's one.** "Pay once, ~$25, no subscription" is a clean, uncontested position against the current market leader. Their own review corpus supplies the ammunition: the single most-upvoted Play review on the listing today (1,441 helpful) is a **billing complaint** about a trial that charged after a failed cancellation, and the current top-of-page reviews include another user calling the subscription flow "deceptive." Subscription resentment is their soft underbelly.

**Distribution economics:** they sell **direct on the web via Stripe** (card, Google Pay, Apple Pay, Link) as well as through Apple/Google, and their help centre explicitly warns that store purchases carry "extra taxes or platform fees." They are already routing around the 15–30% store cut where they can.

## 6. AI cost model

No public disclosure of models or vendors. What can be inferred from behaviour:

- **The pipeline is a cascade, cheapest-first.** Caption text → LLM structuring (cheap). No caption → **audio transcription** (ASR — meaningfully more expensive, and the step that fails most). Still nothing → **web search + scrape + parse** the original source. They only pay for the expensive path when the cheap one fails. `[UNVERIFIED]` which vendors — plausibly OpenAI/Gemini for structuring plus a Whisper-class ASR, but nothing is published.
- **They do not process video frames.** Confirmed by their own troubleshooting article: caption, audio, or web — never vision-on-video. That's the single biggest cost saving they've made, and it's also their biggest accuracy gap (see §8).
- **OCR** for cookbook/handwriting is a separate, per-image vision call.
- **"Ask ReciMe"** is an open-ended chat surface over recipe context — unbounded turns, the least predictable cost line they have. Notably it is Plus-only, i.e. rate-limited by paywall rather than by credit budget.
- **The cap mechanism is blunt: 5 imports/week free, unlimited paid.** No credit system, no token meter, no per-feature quota. Unlimited-on-paid is a real cost risk for them if power users bulk-import libraries — but the weekly free cap plus a paid ARPU of ~$40/yr suggests import volume per user is low enough that it works.

**Relevance to our one-time ~$25:** their model tells us a caption-first cascade keeps average per-import cost low, and that unlimited AI import at ~$40/user/year is survivable for a funded team. For a one-time $25 with no recurring revenue, the same architecture is workable **only** if we (a) do caption/HTML/JSON-LD parsing first and hit ASR/vision rarely, and (b) put a fair-use ceiling on the expensive paths. Their cascade is the design to copy.

## 8. Gaps and weaknesses — concrete

1. **No pantry. At all.** Grocery Checkout literally asks the user to "deselect pantry staples… you already have at home" by hand, every single order. No inventory, no expiry, no deduction. Their own founder conceded Paprika beat them here in 2023 and it is still true in 2026. This is the single largest hole in the "recipes → plan → shop → cook" loop.
2. **No food diary, no macro tracking, no health integration.** Nutrition is a per-recipe calculator, still in **beta**, with no daily log, no targets, no Health Connect/Apple Health. Users have been asking for MyFitnessPal linkage since at least Jan 2025 and got nothing. There is no "did I hit my protein today" surface anywhere in the product.
3. **The grocery list is dumb.** No duplicate merging (even for the same recipe twice in one week), no unit summing across recipes (1 cup + 20 ml soy sauce stays two lines), no editable or reorderable aisle categories, no way to move a mis-filed item. Users have been reporting this since 2023.
4. **Meal plan and shopping list are not wired together.** Change a recipe or a serving count on the plan and the list does not update — you must remember to regenerate. Plan to Eat calls this out as the main reason ReciMe "feels like a beginner version."
5. **Meal plan is one week only.** No fortnight, no month, no multi-week view.
6. **AI fails predictably in four named cases** (their own troubleshooting page): private accounts and login-walled sites; recipes posted in the **comments** rather than the caption; posts containing **multiple recipes** (only one is grabbed); and — critically — **video where the recipe is only shown visually, with no caption and nothing usable in the audio.** They read captions, they transcribe audio, they never watch the video. Every workaround they offer is "take a screenshot and import that as an image," i.e. make the user do the work.
7. **Android is a second-class citizen, and they say so.** Shared cookbooks and all collaboration are **iOS-only** as of 2026-03-05. Android launched without import or subscriptions at all. The store description still lists "Access Across Multiple Devices – iOS and iPad." A 3:1 iOS:Android rating ratio confirms it.
8. **Library management is weak.** No alphabetical sort, no newest-first sort, no cross-library filter by ingredient. The top-voted recent Play review is exactly this complaint. Once a user has a few hundred recipes, the app gets worse, not better — the opposite of what a keeper app should do.
9. **Localisation is thin.** 5 languages, none Nordic. No Norwegian, Danish, Swedish, Finnish, Icelandic, Dutch, Italian or Polish. Grocery Checkout partners are US-only. Norwegian iOS ratings: **292**. Northern Europe is effectively unclaimed.
10. **No timers, no widgets, no watch app, no iPad landscape.** All requested for years.
11. **No offline story.** Cloud-first with no published offline behaviour — bad in a kitchen with a weak signal, and bad for a "your recipes forever" promise.
12. **No bulk export.** PDF/print is per-recipe and paywalled. Your library is not portable — a real lock-in complaint waiting to be exploited.
13. **Billing friction is their reputational sore spot.** The most-upvoted review on the Play listing (1,441 helpful) is a user charged after a cancellation that silently failed; another current top review calls the flow "deceptive." Trial-to-charge is where they bleed goodwill.
14. **No PDF import, no real migration path** from Paprika/Mela/Notion. Switching *in* is manual copy-paste.
15. **Minimum iOS 17** excludes a lot of the older kitchen iPads their own users describe.

## 9. What we should steal — ranked

**Steal, highest value first:**

1. **The caption → audio → source-website import cascade.** Cheapest path first, escalate only on failure, always keep the original link. This is the single best engineering idea in the product and it is publicly documented. Then **beat it**: add the frame-level vision pass they refuse to do, so recipes that only exist visually in a video still import. That is their named, admitted failure case.
2. **Ruthless single-action focus at onboarding.** They rebuilt the entire app around one action — import — and tripled retention. First-run should get a user from "I have a TikTok link" to "I have a clean recipe card" in under 30 seconds, with nothing else in the way.
3. **Share-sheet-first ingestion.** Register on the Android share sheet for every source app and make one tap the whole flow. Their #1 onboarding tip is literally "add ReciMe to your share shortcuts."
4. **Inline ingredient quantities in the method steps.** Ingredients highlighted in the instructions; tap to see the scaled/converted amount without scrolling back. Small, cheap, genuinely loved.
5. **Auto-detected oven temperature conversion** — tap the orange temperature text to flip C/F. Same class of small win. Give it away free; they charge for conversion now.
6. **Aisle-sorted grocery list with a sort-by-recipe toggle.** Table stakes — but then fix the four things they never did: merge duplicates, sum units across recipes, let users rename/reorder aisles, and **link the list live to the meal plan**.
7. **Preserve the original source link on every import**, and credit the creator. Users specifically praise this; it also defuses the "you're scraping creators" attack.
8. **Founder-in-the-thread product research.** Her OzBargain threads produced OCR scanning, shared cookbooks and a dozen fixes for the price of a few evenings answering comments. Cheapest roadmap input available.
9. **Direct web checkout via Stripe alongside the store IAP.** They already do it. For a one-time ~$25 product, keeping the full amount on web sales instead of losing 15–30% is material.
10. **Cook mode that keeps the screen awake app-wide** — free, universally praised, trivial to build.

**Beat them here (their gaps are our differentiators):**
- Pantry inventory with expiry, wired to auto-deduct from the shopping list. They have literally nothing.
- Food diary + macro tracking + Health Connect. They have nothing.
- Library management: alphabetical, newest-first, filter-by-ingredient across the whole library. Their top complaint.
- Multi-week meal planning.
- Full offline access — a one-time purchase should mean your recipes work on a plane.
- Bulk library export, free. "Your data is yours" is the natural counter-position to a subscription incumbent.
- Norwegian and Nordic localisation. 292 Norwegian iOS ratings is an open goal.
- Full Android-first parity. Everything they treat as iOS-only, we ship on Android first.

**Avoid:**
- **Don't copy the paywall creep.** They now charge for a *unit converter* and a *print button*. That is the behaviour that generates their worst reviews, and undercutting it is our entire pitch.
- **Don't copy the identical-boilerplate release notes.** Twenty-five releases in six months, and not one told a user what changed. Cheap trust, cheaply thrown away.
- **Don't copy the off-topic SEO blog.** "Best Day Trading Apps" on a recipe domain reads as spam and risks the brand.
- **Don't copy trial-with-card-required-then-auto-charge.** It's the source of their loudest complaints. A one-time price makes this moot — say so loudly.
- **Don't copy "free lifetime" giveaway stunts** to farm chart rank. It works, but it directly cannibalises a one-time-purchase business in a way it doesn't cannibalise a subscription one.
- **Don't build a social/discovery feed.** They classified themselves under Social Networking and then abandoned discovery entirely, because organising beat discovering. That lesson cost them a rebuild; take it for free.
