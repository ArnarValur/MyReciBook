## Positioning

**The pitch has been rewritten twice, and the second rewrite is the important one.**

1. **2016–2022 — budget:** "feed 4 or more for under £10". Student-origin, cost-of-living, five cookbooks all carrying that subtitle. Free recipes on the web and social; revenue from brand partnerships. Lebus: for the first six to seven years the commercial relationship "was with partners rather than users" ([Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/news/ben-lebus-to-headline-business-of-apps-london-2026-with-mobs-journey-to-the-uks-leading-recipe-app/)).
2. **2023 — membership:** Mob Premium as a *bonus tier* — exclusive recipes, meal plans, an online magazine, brand discounts, "all that for less than the price of a pint" ([Introducing Mob Premium, 22 Aug 2023](https://www.mob.co.uk/life/introducing-mob-plus)).
3. **2025–2026 — weeknight logistics:** the current pitch. The job-to-be-done Lebus presented on stage: *"When weekday meals keep coming, I want a reliable, low-effort way to choose what to cook, and cook what I've chosen, so I can eat well, without stress."*

**Critically: the budget framing has been dropped from the product.** Neither store listing mentions cost, price per portion, or "under £10" anywhere. The health framing replaced it — "EAT HEALTHIER, NO COMPROMISE… full nutrition info and macros, including high protein". Mob repositioned from *cheap* to *organised and high-protein*. Target user moved from broke students to time-poor households — "the main cook of the house".

**Deliberate anti-positioning:** "NO BOXES, NO LOCK IN — unlike a meal kit, there's no rigid box turning up at your door." Mob benchmarks itself against **HelloFresh/Gousto**, not against recipe apps. An App Store reviewer independently frames it that way: "similar to the Gousto boxes… but I prefer the freedom of buying my own ingredients."

**The audience-to-subscriber conversion:** 2.7m Instagram followers were the acquisition pool. Product manager Michael Sladden, Feb 2025: "the key focus has been on converting those 2.7 million Instagram followers — which as a brand that's launching a subscription service is pretty exciting, even envy-inducing." That conversion is now largely spent; paid social is the current engine.

**The abandoned bets** (Lebus, on stage): cookbooks, a food truck, spice mixes, and **Peckish**, an influencer-led second app. "Cutting everything else allowed us to go all in."

## Free vs Membership

**The paywall has moved up steadily and is now near-total.**

| Era | Free | Paywalled |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 – Mar 2023 | The entire recipe library, on web and social | Nothing |
| Aug 2023 (Premium launch) | The main library stayed free | 50 exclusive recipes, meal plans, the Planner, an online magazine, **nutrition info**, **saving recipes**, brand discounts |
| 2026 (observed) | A small minority of recipes carry a **"Free"** tag on the web (roughly 15–20% of homepage tiles). Recipe titles, hero images, ratings, chef bios and user notes are public | **Everything else**: ingredient quantities, method, nutrition, planner, shopping list, saving, collections, Adapt Recipe |

Two observed details sharpen this:

- Even a **"Free"-tagged** recipe page renders the ingredient list **without quantities** and shows the button **"Unlock this free recipe"**. "Free" now means "free once you have an account", not "readable".
- The **app is fully gated** — the 7-day trial is card-required before you reach value. A third-party UX audit reported that 94% of users hit the paywall and only about a third started a trial `[UNVERIFIED — source page (highrise-mob-master-customer-insight.netlify.app) is now dead; figures survive only in search-engine cache]`.

A Play review from 28 Aug 2025 captures the customer-facing effect: "has no free version where [it] certainly used to have some free recipes before" — 45 people marked it helpful.

**How much of the library is locked: effectively all of it for practical use.** Roughly four in five recipes are not even browsable; the remainder are browsable but not cookable without an account.

## Content and commerce

- **Recipe development is fully in-house.** A team of salaried chefs working from a **test kitchen in Hackney/Shoreditch**. Every recipe carries a named chef with a bio page. This is a real editorial cost base (31 staff) and the source of the "reliable, chef-tested" claim.
- **Cadence:** new recipes weekly; ~2,000–3,000 total after a decade.
- **Cookbooks (5, HarperCollins/Pavilion):** *MOB Kitchen* (2018), *MOB Veggie*, *Speedy MOB* (12-minute meals), *Comfort MOB*, *Earth MOB* (food waste / environmental). All the early ones carry the "feed 4 or more for under £10" subtitle. Publishing has been **wound down** as a strategic distraction.
- **Sponsored / branded content:** was the entire business model for six to seven years. Named partners include **Spotify** (cooking content with music/podcast talent) and **Aldi**. A "Healthy by ZOE" recipe byline appears in the current library, indicating a live nutrition-brand partnership.
- **Supermarket partnerships:** an Aldi relationship existed on the media side, but there is **no basket/checkout integration** in the product. Mob positions against lock-in: "You buy your own ingredients, your way, at your own supermarket."
- **Merch, food truck, spice mixes, the Peckish app:** all discontinued.
- **Live events:** none current found.

## Business signals

**Growth:**
- 100,000 → **280,000** paying subscribers, Feb 2025 → Apr 2026.
- **$12.5M ARR** (Apr 2026), against £2.5m turnover in Dec 2022 — a roughly 4× revenue expansion driven entirely by the subscription pivot.
- **#1 top grossing Food & Drink in the UK** on Play, sustained.
- ~510 Android installs/day and rising international top-15 grossing placements (Finland, Denmark, Belgium, Czechia, Slovakia, Mexico, Indonesia, India).
- **Apple recognition:** the app claims "Apple App of the Day and featured by Apple" in its own App Store copy. This is a self-declaration, not an Apple Design Award — Mob does not appear in Apple's 2025 or 2026 Design Award lists.
- Lebus was the **opening keynote at Business of Apps London 2026** (23 Apr 2026).

**Strain:**
- Last filed accounts: **negative equity of £1.30m**, cumulative losses **£2.32m**, cash down to **£0.59m**, **headcount down 39 → 31**.

## Why the low raw rating (4.16 lifetime, 180 one-stars)

These are structural product and business-model causes, not sentiment. The reviews agent covers tone; this is the machinery.

1. **The trial-to-annual conversion trap — the single largest cause.** A card-gated 7-day trial that auto-converts to a **£39.99 annual** charge. Reviewers repeatedly describe forgetting to cancel, being charged a full year, and being refused a refund ("Scammed into a year subscription", App Store, 2 Jun 2026; "charged me for a full year… they wouldn't refund me at all", Play, 12 Apr 2026). Mob's stock reply is that the terms were disclosed. This converts *billing* dissatisfaction into *product* one-stars.
2. **Retroactive paywalling of a library that was free for seven years.** A 4-million-follower audience built on free content watched that content disappear behind a wall between 2023 and 2026. The most-upvoted negative review (45 helpful votes) is exactly this.
3. **Content quantity and sameness versus the price.** ~2,000–3,000 recipes for £39.99/yr, from a brand whose recipes are stylistically consistent by design. "The recipes are all very samey… Can get better and more variety for free from many food creators on social media."
4. **Broken ingredient taxonomy → allergen filter failures.** A reviewer with peanut and sesame allergies filtered both out and every suggested recipe still contained them, because "peanut" does not subsume "salted peanut", "smooth peanut butter", etc. (Play, 22 Apr 2026). Called "unusable and dangerous". This is a normalisation defect in the ingredient model.
5. **Localisation defects.** Metric/imperial preference ignored, US-oriented units and instructions shown to UK users (Play, 13 Jul 2026). The paying customer's membership was then cancelled rather than the bug fixed.
6. **Android and tablet neglect.** No iPad build at all. A paying Android customer reports being unable to install on a Samsung tablet. Android shipped a full year or more after the web product.
7. **App-behind-web feature debt.** Cook mode and recipe videos existed on the web but were missing from the app from launch until spring 2026 — an 18-month window of paying users getting less on mobile.

## Gaps and weaknesses we can exploit

1. **No personal cookbook.** You cannot add your own recipe. Not one. Mob is a closed editorial catalogue — your grandmother's recipe has nowhere to live.
2. **No import.** No URL, no photo, no social-video capture. Everything the user already has stays outside the app.
3. **No pantry inventory.** "Search by what's in your fridge" is a *search box you type into*, not a tracked pantry. Nothing decrements, nothing expires, nothing warns.
4. **No food diary.** Nutrition and macros are printed on the recipe as reference and then thrown away. Nothing is logged, nothing trends, no daily total.
5. **The budget feature is gone.** The brand that invented "feed 4 for under £10" ships no cost-per-portion anywhere in the app. There is a vacated position with a decade of brand equity attached to it.
6. **UK-centric everything.** English only. UK ingredient names, UK supermarket aisle ordering in the shopping list, UK-normal produce. In Norway this is a foreign-language app that sorts your list into the aisles of a shop you do not have — and it still charges 499 kr/yr.
7. **Allergen filtering is unsafe.** Ingredient strings are not normalised into a taxonomy. This is a correctness bug that a well-modelled ingredient graph fixes outright.
8. **No offline.** Nothing documented, no help article. A recipe app that needs a connection in a kitchen.
9. **No iPad build; Android tablet unreliable.**
10. **No family plan, no household sharing.** One login at a time. Two adults cooking from one meal plan need two subscriptions.
11. **Content quantity per pound.** ~2,000–3,000 recipes at £39.99/yr, growing weekly, versus a one-time purchase.
12. **Recurring-billing resentment is a permanent tax on their rating.** Their pricing model *manufactures* one-star reviews at a steady rate. Ours cannot.
13. **No export.** Your saved collections, plans and notes cannot leave.

## What we should steal — and what to avoid

### Steal

**1. The content→install funnel, exactly as they ran it.** This is directly applicable to Arnar's blog plan:
- Publish genuinely free, genuinely good recipes on the open web first. Mob spent **seven years** giving content away before asking for a penny, and turned 1m→2.7m Instagram followers into 100k subscribers.
- Start the paywall **absurdly small**. Mob Premium launched with **50 recipes** behind it, framed as a bonus, not a wall. Don't lead with a locked door.
- Named human authors with bios on every recipe. Mob's chefs have profile pages. It builds the trust that makes a stranger pay.
- Community notes under every recipe, with staff replying. Free UGC, free SEO, free proof the recipe works. Cheap for us to build, and it feeds the "will this actually work" anxiety that drives conversion.

**2. Their winning ad script — the anti-blog pitch.** After six months of testing, the ad that beat everything ("Evergreen Adam") was a plain testimonial whose sharpest line is the differentiator against free recipes online:

> "The recipes have no ads, no long back stories and each ingredient amount is shown in the method. So you don't need to scroll mid-cook."

That is our pitch verbatim, and we can make it *more* honestly than Mob can — a native app with a one-time purchase has no ads and no upsell, ever. Their full script structure is worth copying beat for beat: who it's for → what it is + the pain → USPs → when to use it → differentiator vs free recipes → ease → pain resolved → CTA.

**3. Cost-per-portion — pick up what they dropped.** "Feed 4 for under £10" built a brand and five cookbooks, and the app now ships nothing like it. With a pantry and a grocery list we can compute real cost per portion and cost per week. This is an unclaimed, proven, emotionally resonant position and the incumbent has walked away from it.

**4. The "Adapt Recipe" pattern.** Swap an ingredient, raise the protein, cut the calories, convert to vegan, switch to an air fryer. It is the single best feature in their product and it turns a static catalogue into something responsive. Worth building.

**5. Aisle-sorted shopping lists**, with aisle order the user can reorder for *their* shop — which fixes Mob's UK-only assumption for free.

**6. Design language.** Big, warm, confident food photography; short recipe titles; visible cook time; a clean rail-based home. It is the reason a media brand converts at all. Match the visual quality bar.

**7. Their JTBD sentence.** "When weekday meals keep coming, I want a reliable, low-effort way to choose what to cook and cook what I've chosen, so I can eat well, without stress." That is the correct spine for a meal planner and it took Mob nine years to write down.

### Avoid

- **The card-gated trial that auto-converts to an annual charge.** It is demonstrably the largest generator of their one-star reviews and of "scam" language in public. Our one-time price is the answer to this and should be stated loudly and early: *pay once, we can never bill you again.*
- **Removing something that was free.** Every retroactive lock cost them goodwill they had spent seven years accumulating.
- **A closed catalogue.** A recipe app the user cannot put their own recipes into has a ceiling, and Mob is sitting on it.
- **Unnormalised ingredient strings.** Their allergen filter is dangerous because of it. Model ingredients properly from day one.
- **Shipping the app a year behind the web, then leaving features off it.** Eighteen months of paying mobile users getting less than web users is visible in their reviews to this day.
- **English-only in a European market while charging European prices.**
- **Claiming "unlimited"** — Mob's own copy says "Unlimited access to 3000+ recipes". Per our own principle, we don't use that word.
