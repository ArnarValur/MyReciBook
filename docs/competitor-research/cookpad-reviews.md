# Cookpad — Reviews, Complaints & Unmet Needs

_Researched: 2026-09-01_

## Scope and method

Two Play listings were examined, plus five App Store storefronts.

- **Listing A — "Cookpad recipes, homemade food"**, package `com.mufumbo.android.recipe.search`, developer **Cookpad Inc (UK)** (Bristol), 10m+ installs, Editors' choice, updated 31 Aug 2026. This is the global/Western app.
- **Listing B — "Cookpad: Tasty Food Recipes"**, package `com.cookpad.android.activities`, developer **Cookpad Inc.** (Tokyo), 10m+ installs. Same package serves the Japanese listing クックパッド -No.1料理レシピ検索アプリ in Japan and an English/India custom listing elsewhere.
- iOS `id585332633` (renamed **"Cookpad: Recipe & Meal Planner"** as of the Aug 2026 release, publisher COOKPAD INC. (CA)) sampled in GB, ES, DE, NO. iOS `id340368403` (Japan) sampled in JP.

Review text was read directly from the Play review dialogs (sorted Newest and Most-relevant, filtered by star band), from Apple's public customer-review RSS (GB), and from App Store review pages (ES/DE/NO/JP). Everything quoted below is verbatim from a real review unless marked `[SECONDARY]`. Non-English quotes carry a translation. Apple's review RSS returned empty for US/ES/JP storefronts on the observation date, so those markets were read from the web review pages instead.

**Recency trend — the global listing is the one decaying.**

Counting the star value of every written review in the "Newest" stream:

- **Listing A (US storefront), 60 newest written reviews spanning 19 Apr – 26 Aug 2026: mean 4.03.** Breakdown 5★ 39, 4★ 7, 3★ 2, 2★ 1, **1★ 11 (18.3%)**. Lifetime US mean is 4.57 with a 6.3% 1★ share — so the recent 1★ rate is roughly **2.9× the lifetime rate**, and the recent mean is half a star below lifetime.
- **Listing B (JP storefront), 160 newest written reviews spanning 28 Jun – 31 Aug 2026: mean 4.58.** Breakdown 5★ 103, 4★ 47, 3★ 10, 2★ 0, 1★ 0. Lifetime JP mean is 4.54 — **flat, no decay**.

Caveat on method: written reviews over-represent motivated users versus the silent rating-only majority, and the two samples cover different windows. Directionally, though, the split is stark. The Japanese product is stable and beloved at home; the global product is where the anger is, and where the anger is getting louder. The 11% one-star wall on the GB listing is a polarised distribution, not a soft 4.4.

Two further structural facts worth recording. First, the same 1★ mass on Listing A is dominated by reviews from **2021 and 2023–24** — i.e. the paywall retrofit — which means the lifetime 4.4/4.6 is propped up by a decade of pre-paywall five stars. Second, the India rating on Listing B (**3.6**) is a full point below its Japanese rating: non-Japanese users landing on the Japanese package rate it very badly.

## 3. What people hate

Ranked by volume and vote weight.

**1. The paywall retrofit — features that used to be free were taken away (top-voted complaint by a wide margin)**
The 1★ pool on Listing A is dominated by long-term users who watched popularity-sorted search, advanced filters and unlimited saving move behind Premium in 2021 and again in 2023.
- "Now I have to pay a monthly fee to get THE BEST SEARCH RESULTS!" — Jay H, Play, 2 Jan 2024 (**215 found helpful** — the single most-upvoted 1★)
- "holds hostage my oldest saved recipes, unless I subscribe" — Zsófia Mészáros, Play, 18 Jun 2021 (110 helpful)
- "You can't even sort by most popular without paying." — Tom R, Play (Listing B), 14 Sep 2025 (6 helpful)
- "This app is nothing more than a money grabber." — Jake Wright, Play (Listing B), 5 Jul 2025 (35 helpful)

Cookpad's own replies confirm the mechanics: free users may bookmark **a maximum of 60** recipes; Premium reorders search by most-cooked first; all recipes remain viewable. The gap between that (accurate) framing and how users experience it is the whole problem — many reviewers sincerely believe recipes themselves are paywalled ("You need to pay to use this app" — Mariqn (Mimo), Play, 17 May 2026; Cookpad replied correcting it).

**2. The save cap punishes contributors, not just consumers**
- "Eso es una estafa y un atentado a nuestra inteligencia" ("That's a scam and an insult to our intelligence") — Dos.Pi, iOS ES, 3 Jul 2023, a user who had published 150+ recipes for free and was then limited in how many others' recipes he could save
- "Why did you take all of the features away? … at least grandfathered some of us in" — Alice Hatter, Play, 30 Jun 2023 (43 helpful)

**3. Login is broken, and has been for years**
The most common single functional failure across every market and both listings, spanning 2014 → 2026.
- "Just freezes at the login screen and says your logged in" — Shawn Mahoney, Play, 21 May 2026 (7 helpful)
- "cannot login with any account" — Rakai Daksa, Play, 21 Feb 2026 (3 helpful)
- "no me deja iniciar sesion con gogle" ("won't let me sign in with Google") — Hernan Grinstein, Play, 22 Mar 2026
- "When I try to login with Facebook I don't even get the login screen" — epic FUZZ, iOS GB, 26 Apr 2026
- "Leider funktioniert die App nicht … nach der Anmeldung nicht mehr weiter" ("the app doesn't work … after sign-in it goes no further") — Hello_Tweety, iOS DE, 11 Aug 2019

Also resented: login is mandatory at all. "cannot use without logging in" — Jyonn Bzomu, Play, 19 Nov 2025 (3 helpful).

**4. Subscription cancellation friction (accusation-heavy; report as such)**
This is the loudest theme in the Japanese 1★ stream and appears in Europe too.
- "騙された感が否めないです。" ("I can't shake the feeling of being deceived") — H U, Play JP, 17 Jan 2026 (**48 helpful**), describing a birthday free month they could not find any way to cancel before it auto-renewed
- "解約できません" ("I can't cancel") — TK, Play JP, 6 Mar 2026
- "Premium cannot be cancelled." — Máté Koós, Play, 29 Jan 2026 (13 helpful). He alleges the cancellation link served an ad on a timer and then a dead page, and says he may report it to authorities. **This is an unverified user accusation.**
- A Japanese user wrote out the full Play Store cancellation procedure in a review to help others — KENGO, Play JP, 27 Jan 2026 (35 helpful)

Partly structural, and Cookpad says so itself: the Japanese App Store listing states that Premium bought through iTunes **cannot be cancelled from the Cookpad app or website at all**, and that mid-month cancellation is not accepted. That is a platform reality, but it lands on users as a trap. `[SECONDARY]` Japanese Q&A boards carry the same complaint from users who were still being charged.

**5. Untested, low-quality UGC — and the paywall makes it worse**
The sharpest formulation of the structural problem with UGC at scale:
- "leaving you to sort through all the recipes that are untested or questionable" — MrDedlockheart, Play, 3 Nov 2024 (17 helpful), on the best recipes being ranked behind Premium

- "There are not any decent recipes." — Timothy Herren, Play, 20 Jun 2026. Cookpad's reply concedes the point: recipes "are shared by home cooks from around the world, so the content can vary widely."

## 4. Deal-breakers — where users churn and what they move to

The exit is almost always triggered by the same event: a long-term user hits a limit that did not used to exist.

**Trigger 1 — Popularity sort / best results moved behind Premium.**
"It's time to find a new recipe app, I shouldn't have to pay to get the best results." — Jay H, Play, 2 Jan 2024 (215 helpful)

**Trigger 2 — The 60-save cap hitting an existing collection.**
"I will activate the free temporary subscription, save the recipes by screenshots, and then immediately delete the app." — Zsófia Mészáros, Play, 18 Jun 2021 (110 helpful)

**Trigger 3 — Login failure.** Several reviewers uninstall rather than debug: "Alright, uninstall for now." — Care Joewono, Play, 1 Mar 2025.

**Where they go.** Named destinations, in order of frequency:
1. **Pinterest** — "you can just go to Pinterest or literally any search engine" (Ernest Figueroa, Play, 26 Jan 2024, 72 helpful); "I guess Pinterest would be the top gear from now on" (Reyes Rye, Play, 2 Jul 2023, 60 helpful)
2. **Plain web search** — "resulta más conveniente buscar recetas por google y no descargar la aplicación" ("it's more convenient to search Google and not download the app") — Agus M, Play, 16 Feb 2026
3. **Rival recipe apps that give popularity sorting free** — in Japan: "他のレシピアプリだと無料で人気検索できる" ("other recipe apps let you search by popularity for free") — ☆み☆る☆く☆, iOS JP, 20 Jun 2019, a 10-year Premium member threatening to quit. Play's own "similar apps" rail in Japan surfaces DelishKitchen (4.5), Kurashiru (4.3) and Nadia (4.6).
4. **Nothing specific — just "there are better ones"** in Europe: "Es gibt bessere." — Lananas007, iOS DE, 2 Apr 2021

The counter-view exists and is well-upvoted: "Sucks premium cost money but gotta make money" — Joe carroll, Play, 17 May 2024 (**108 helpful**), who argues free search still surfaces good recipes with effort. He does confirm you cannot save recipes to the phone.

## 6. Non-Japanese / European experience

**The content is regionally siloed, and users notice.** Three independent reviewers across six years describe the same wall:
- "you can only find recipes from people in the same region" — Maz Maz, Play, 2 Jun 2020 (28 helpful)
- "Why is it assumed that I like to see recipes of my country only?" — Suryaprakash Vankamamidi, Play, 30 Jul 2020
- "I can't change the language when I change the country. In Italy, all recipes are in Italian" — Nataliia10, iOS DE, 24 May 2025

So the "millions of recipes worldwide" pitch resolves, for any individual European user, to whatever their own small national community has posted. Spain, at 220,000 recipes as of Cookpad's own 2017 reply to a Spanish reviewer, is one of the *strong* European markets.

**Two apps, one brand, and users land on the wrong one.** Non-Japanese users who install `com.cookpad.android.activities` get a Japanese app:
- "Everything is in Japanese. I don't speak that language. Not very useful" — Bradley Gaarder, Play, 4 Feb 2025 (7 helpful)
- "How do I get to the English variation? I can't read Japanese unfortunately." — Natia E, Play, 21 Dec 2020 (**23 helpful**)
- "Poor localisation. It's not hard; This is the 'Australian' GP Store." — Jared Lane, Play, 24 Oct 2022 (2 helpful)

That confusion is a plausible driver of the **3.6** India rating on Listing B versus **4.5** in Japan.

**The Nordics do not exist for Cookpad.**
- The App Store's own metadata lists 25 supported languages — AR, BN, EN, FR, DE, EL, GU, HA, HI, HU, ID, IT, JA, MS, MR, FA, PL, PT, RO, ES, TA, TH, ZH, UK, VI. **No Norwegian, Swedish, Danish, Finnish, Icelandic or Dutch.**
- Cookpad Premium is sold in Arabic-speaking countries, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, France, Greece, Hungary, India (English only), Indonesia, Italy, Malaysia, Mexico, Poland, Romania, Spain, Taiwan, Thailand, Ukraine, UK, US, Uruguay and Vietnam — **no Nordic country, no Germany, no Netherlands**. `[SECONDARY]` reporting notes users outside the list simply never see the option ([Pann, 2026](https://www.pann-app.com/blog/cookpad-review)). The 60-save cap, however, is global: a Norwegian user hits the ceiling with no way to pay past it.
- iOS Norway holds **51 lifetime ratings**. The three reviews shown on that storefront are in Hungarian, English and Russian. **Zero Norwegian-language reviews.** Germany holds 755.

**Translation quality.** Cookpad does not machine-translate the corpus into your language; it routes you to a language community. One recent Spanish reviewer praises a new capability — "ahora se pueden importar recetas de otras app y las traduce" ("now you can import recipes from other apps and it translates them") — Kokoroana, iOS ES, ~28 Aug 2026. That is import-time translation, not catalogue translation.

**Metric handling.** No unit conversion in either direction (§3.9). The corpus carries whatever units the poster used, so a European user browsing English-language content meets US cups with no conversion.

**GDPR and data.** The Play data-safety declarations for the two listings differ sharply:
- **Listing A (Cookpad Inc (UK))** declares it *may share with third parties*: Financial info (purchase history), Personal info (user IDs), crash logs, app interactions, device IDs, **and photos and videos**. It declares collection of messages, name, email, user IDs, approximate location, user-generated content, device IDs, photos and videos.
- **Listing B (Cookpad Inc., Tokyo)** declares **no data shared with third parties**.

Both offer a data-deletion request route. User-side friction was reported: no in-app account deletion and a **privacy policy served only in English** to a German user — "Datenschutzbestimmungen auf englisch." (Lananas007, iOS DE, 2 Apr 2021). Under GDPR that is at minimum an Art. 12 intelligibility problem for a consumer service marketed in Germany. Another reviewer docked a star specifically over permissions: "they want access to my personal phone information" — Rose Al-Sawmahi, Play, 28 Nov 2021.

**Market retrenchment.** Cookpad has not, on the evidence found, shut a country service and stranded users outright. It has, however, retreated hard: it ran recipe services in **73 countries and 31 languages** at end-2022, then in 2023 cut **80 overseas staff** alongside 40 domestic redundancies and the closure of five domestic side businesses, after two consecutive loss-making years. Japanese business press describes a continuous decline since 2016. The thinness European users report is the visible edge of that retreat, not neglect of a growing operation.

## 7. Trust signals

**The "recipe scrap" controversy (March 2026) — the most serious trust event in Cookpad's recent history.**
Cookpad shipped a feature letting users paste a URL from Instagram, X or a personal recipe site; AI reads the ingredients and steps and files them into the user's private records. Free members get **5 imports per week**, paid members unlimited. Beginning 21 March 2026, prominent Japanese food creators objected publicly:
- Ryuji (@ore825), 21 Mar 2026: "あまりにもレシピ製作者にリスペクトがない" ("there is far too little respect for recipe creators")
- 白ごはん.com's 冨田ただすけ, 21 Mar 2026, said he would prefer users not use it, because a personally-run recipe site carries heavy fixed costs and a feature like this from a major platform makes recipe publishing unsustainable

The backlash reached the store. Four separate 1★ Play reviews in March 2026 in Japanese:
- "レシピ製作者へのリスペクトが足りないアプリです" ("an app that lacks respect for recipe creators") — 村泰, 25 Mar 2026, who also flags the AI summarisation angle
- "モラル無さすぎる" ("completely amoral") — Akiya Tanaka, 21 Mar 2026 (3 helpful)
- "倫理観がおわっています" ("their ethics are finished") — AAA AAA, 21 Mar 2026, objecting that the original creator gets nothing while Cookpad charges for volume
- A fourth accuses Cookpad of reducing the source's impressions by keeping viewing inside its own app — ブロードキャスティング, 22 Mar 2026

**These are accusations, and the legal position is contested.** Ingredient lists are generally not copyrightable; prose and photographs can be. Cookpad published a position on 22 March 2026 stating the feature is for personal record-keeping with no publication or redistribution, that it links back to the original, and that it would review the specification. Note the countervailing user view: European reviewers *praise* the same feature ("you can import recipes from social media" — Kyriakos Kromlidis, Play, 31 May 2026, 14 helpful; Kokoroana, iOS ES, Aug 2026). **Directly relevant to MyReciBook, which will also import recipes.**

**Recipe safety from untested UGC — structural and unresolved.** Cookpad's own reply to a 1★ review states plainly that content "can vary widely" because it is home-cook contributed. There is no testing, no editorial pass, and — outside Premium — no popularity ranking to act as a proxy for "this actually works." A reviewer's request for "more strict regulations for who can post" (71 helpful) has gone unanswered for six years. Early on, the platform had a downvote-to-remove mechanic that a 2015 German reviewer described approvingly; nothing equivalent is visible now.

**Moderation quality — mixed, with two ugly anecdotes.** Cookpad does curate: an Indonesian user's 1★ complaint that their recipe never appeared drew a reply confirming recipes pass through curation before surfacing (Agnes Margaret Meling / Cookpad reply, Aug 2026). Against that, two 1★ iOS GB reviews allege moderator overreach — unsolicited admin DMs continuing after being blocked, followed by a ban (Tom Gudge, 25 Aug 2022), and a copyright claim made against a user's own recipe (Alfie chaplin, 5 Apr 2022). **Both are one-sided user accounts with no corroboration.** One positive counter-report: "Admin are always there to help support and keep you safe" (Tottenham lady, iOS GB, 3 Nov 2021).

**Developer responsiveness — genuinely excellent, and the strongest thing in Cookpad's favour.** Cookpad Inc (UK) replies to the large majority of Play reviews including 1★ ones, typically within days, **in the reviewer's own language** (English, Indonesian, Spanish, Malay observed). Japanese support replies are specific and technical, naming the broken build number and the fix. It converts: "Extra star for swift response to my review" — Jonny Duffy, Play, 30 Jul 2026. The reverse is also visible — replies sometimes ask users to raise their star rating, which reads as rating-farming.

**No Trustpilot presence** for cookpad.com was found on the observation date.

## 8. What this hands us

Split into what Cookpad **cannot** fix without breaking its own model, and what is simply neglect.

### Structural — where a UGC giant cannot follow us

1. **Recipe trustworthiness.** Cookpad's scale is the problem: millions of untested recipes with no editorial floor, and the one proxy for quality — popularity ranking — is sold as a subscription. Our lever is not "more recipes"; it is **a curated, tested base set that we stand behind**, plus deterministic sort/filter for free. Their own reviewer wrote the line for us: users are left "to sort through all the recipes that are untested or questionable."
2. **Nutrition, food diary and tracking.** Cookpad has none of this outside a legacy Japanese calorie feature. It is not a small gap — it is a different product. **Our whole nutrition + diary + pantry stack is uncontested territory.**
3. **Regional silos.** Their content is fragmented by country/language, which is precisely why Europe feels empty. A curated corpus that is the same everywhere and metric-native does not have that failure mode.
4. **The subscription itself.** Every top-voted grievance — save caps, popularity sort, filters, folders, import quotas, cancellation friction — descends from recurring revenue. A **one-time ~$25 purchase deletes the entire category of complaint at a stroke**, including the "I published 150 recipes and you still cap me" contributor grievance. Do not build any cap that would ever need lifting for money.
5. **The Nordics.** No Nordic language, no Premium availability, 51 iOS ratings in Norway. Cookpad is not competing in Arnar's home region at all. That is either an open flank or a warning about market size — worth deciding deliberately rather than by default.

### Fixable neglect — cheap wins they have left on the table for years

6. **Offline access to your own saved recipes.** Requested since 2018 in three languages, still absent. Trivial for a local-first app; a genuine differentiator in a kitchen with bad wifi.
7. **Unit conversion (cups ⇄ g/ml/oz) and servings scaling.** Asked for since 2016, still nothing. For a European audience this is table stakes and Cookpad fails it outright.
8. **A saved-recipe library that survives scale** — folders, search inside saves, tried/untried state, no "same 20 recipes" pagination bug. Their most-upvoted 4★ review is exactly this failure. Ours must be flawless at 1,000+ saves and must never be metered.
9. **Login that works, and no forced account.** Seven straight years of login-failure reviews across every market. Local-first with optional sync, and **usable before sign-in**, closes a wound they have not stopped bleeding from.
10. **Meal plan → automatic grocery list.** Requested from 2019, shipped Aug 2026. We should treat it as baseline, not a feature.
11. **Data durability.** Multiple reports of saved recipes and in-progress drafts vanishing. Draft auto-save, export, and a visible backup story are cheap trust-builders against a competitor with this history.
12. **Account deletion in-app, notification controls, and a privacy policy in the user's language.** Cookpad's UK listing declares it may share purchase history, user IDs and **photos and videos** with third parties; a German user could not delete their account and got an English-only privacy notice. **A clean, plain-language, Norway/EEA-correct privacy posture with no third-party sharing is a real marketing asset against them**, and cheap for a one-time-purchase app that does not need ad-tech.
13. **Ethical recipe import.** Their import feature is genuinely loved by users and genuinely hated by creators. We will face the same tension. The defensible design: **strong visible attribution, a working link back to the source, private-by-default storage, no republication, no quota that makes the import look like a monetised scraping service.** The Japanese backlash landed hardest on the fact that Cookpad *charged* for volume on other people's work — avoid that shape entirely.

### What to copy

14. **Reply to every review, in the reviewer's language, fast.** Cookpad does this and it demonstrably converts angry users into higher ratings. It costs nothing but attention and it is the single clearest thing they do better than most of the category. Do not, however, copy the habit of asking reviewers to raise their star rating.
15. **Ingredient-first search is the killer feature, universally.** It is the top praise theme in every market and language sampled. Make ours better than theirs — pantry-aware, and offline.
16. **Ad-free is a real, felt benefit.** Users explicitly contrast Cookpad with "ad-choked external websites." A paid app is ad-free by construction; say so loudly.
